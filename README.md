# AI/ML Paper Implementations

This repository is a personal learning project focused on developing a deeper understanding of **Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning through implementation**.

Rather than only reading influential papers, my goal is to **implement their core ideas from scratch**, experiment with them, and understand the reasoning, mathematics, and engineering decisions behind them.

## Purpose

The goal of this repository is simple:

> **Learn AI/ML by rebuilding the ideas that shaped it.**

Over time, I will continue adding implementations of prominent AI/ML papers, beginning with fundamental and foundational work and gradually progressing toward more modern architectures, techniques, and research.

The repository serves as both:

* A hands-on learning environment
* A collection of paper implementations
* A record of my progress studying AI/ML
* A place to experiment beyond the original papers
* A reference I can return to as my understanding grows

## What I'll Be Implementing

The repository will gradually cover papers and concepts across areas such as:

**Foundations**

* Linear and logistic models
* Neural networks and backpropagation
* Optimization methods
* Representation learning

**Deep Learning**

* Convolutional Neural Networks
* Recurrent Neural Networks
* LSTMs and sequence modeling
* Attention mechanisms

**Modern AI**

* Transformers
* Large Language Models
* Vision Transformers
* Generative models
* Diffusion models
* Reinforcement learning
* Other influential modern architectures and techniques

The exact roadmap will evolve as I continue learning.

## Repository Philosophy

The implementations in this repository are primarily built for **understanding rather than production**.

Whenever possible, I want to avoid treating frameworks and libraries as black boxes. Implementations may therefore recreate important components manually in order to understand what is actually happening underneath the abstractions.

For each paper, I aim to explore questions such as:

* What problem was the paper trying to solve?
* What existed before it?
* What was the paper's key contribution?
* How does the underlying mathematics work?
* How can the main ideas be implemented?
* Why does the approach work?
* How did the paper influence later AI/ML research?

Some implementations may closely reproduce a paper, while others may focus only on its most important ideas.

As the repository grows, implementations will generally follow a structure similar to:

```text
papers/
├── paper-name/
│   ├── README.md
│   ├── implementation/
│   ├── experiments/
│   └── notes/
│
├── another-paper/
│   ├── README.md
│   ├── implementation/
│   └── experiments/
│
└── ...
```

Individual paper directories may contain:

* Paper summaries
* Architecture explanations
* Mathematical notes
* Implementations
* Training scripts
* Experiments
* Results
* Links to the original paper

## Learning in Public

This repository is intentionally a **work in progress**.

Implementations may change as my understanding improves. Some early implementations may be incomplete, inefficient, or simplified compared with the original research.

That's part of the point.

The goal isn't simply to collect finished implementations—it's to document the process of developing a stronger understanding of AI/ML by working through the research that built the field.

## Long-Term Goal

Over time, I hope this repository becomes a chronological and conceptual journey through influential AI/ML research:

**Foundations → Deep Learning → Attention → Transformers → Modern Generative AI → Current Research**

Each implementation adds another piece to the larger picture of how modern artificial intelligence came to be.
